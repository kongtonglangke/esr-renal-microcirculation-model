[3] Is the sqrt(ESR) scaling an assumption, or is it what the data imply?
    power-law fit  (1+alpha) ~ ESR^q  ->  q = 0.5000
    reference assumption in v0.2.0        q = 0.5000
    maximum deviation of the measured mapping from a pure sqrt law: 0.00 %
    HONEST READING - this is an ALGEBRAIC IDENTITY, not an independent
    validation.  Every step of the chain is linear or proportional:
      V_s = c (1+alpha)^2         (Stokes, single aggregate)
      V_s ~ ESR                   (Westergren relation, measured, r=0.990)
      =>  (1+alpha) ~ sqrt(ESR)   identically.
    What the replacement therefore achieves is NOT a new numerical result
    but a CHANGE IN EPISTEMIC STATUS: the sqrt(ESR) scaling is no longer a
    free assumption; it follows from Stoke's law together with the
    measured near-linear Westergren relation.  The empirical content sits
    in the Westergren linearity (r = 0.990, n = 32), which is measurable
    and falsifiable.  We state this plainly rather than presenting a
    tautological '0 % deviation' as if it were a validation.

[5] Ht sensitivity of the INFERRED aggregation (Higuchi eq 15)
    Eq 15 states  HAI = AI - k (Ht - 0.40), i.e.  AI = HAI + k (Ht - 0.40).
    If an uncorrected AI is read as though it were the HAI, the inferred
    aggregation state is biased by k*dHt, and V_s responds as the 4th
    power of (HAI - b).  At a typical elevated state this is large:
    delta_Ht           Ht    HAI_naive  V_s_bias_factor     pct_bias
      0.0000       0.4000       0.5686           1.0000       0.0000
      0.0200       0.4200       0.5743           1.1691      16.9101
      0.0500       0.4500       0.5828           1.4619      46.1861
      0.0800       0.4800       0.5913           1.8064      80.6440
    -> a patient with Ht 0.45 whose uncorrected aggregation index is read
       as the corrected value will have their aggregation state OVER-
       estimated by about +40-45 %.  This quantitatively supports Sec. 3.7
       of the manuscript ("ESR alone under-determines the rheology") and
       identifies haematocrit, not ESR, as the missing covariate.
